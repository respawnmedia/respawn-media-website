Visual Identity

The Logo

The Respawn logo is a bold, condensed wordmark inside a double-border rectangular frame. The "RE" sits large above "SPAWN" — split by a ruled line. The double-border creates a stamp-like, editorial authority. Think record label. Think streetwear. Think agency.

RE SPAWN
White version — on dark
RE SPAWN
Black version — on light
✓ Correct Usage
  • White logo on black or very dark backgrounds
  • Black logo on white or off-white backgrounds
  • Maintain the double-border rectangle — never use wordmark alone
  • Use as watermark in the corner of all content (top-right preferred)
  • Keep the logo monochrome — only black or white, never teal or colored
  • Preserve all proportions — scale uniformly only
✕ Never Do This
  • Don't stretch, squish, or distort the logo
  • Don't rotate or flip the logo
  • Don't place the logo on a busy, low-contrast background
  • Don't use the logo in teal, gray, or any off-brand color
  • Don't add drop shadows, glows, or effects to the logo
  • Don't recreate or modify the logo from scratch — always use the master file
Clear Space Rule

Always maintain a clear space around the logo equal to at least the height of the "RE" letterforms. Nothing — text, images, graphics — should enter this protected zone. Minimum usable size is 48px height for digital, and 15mm height for print.

Color Usage Rule: The teal accent should never dominate. Use it for one element per design — a stripe, a date, a highlighted word, a border. The more restraint you show with the teal, the more impact it has. When everything is teal, nothing is.

A horizontal teal bar used as a design divider, section separator, or mid-post accent. Always full-width. Never decorative — it always separates something meaningful.

The headline often breaks the grid, runs off edges, or dominates 70–80% of the frame. Text-as-design is core to the Respawn aesthetic. Let the type be loud.

For dark posts, a subtle stone or concrete texture overlays the black background. It adds depth without competing with the content. Never use colored textures.
⬛

Portrait and tribute photography is always desaturated to black and white. This creates gravitas and visual consistency across editorial-style posts.
↓
Directional Arrows
Thin, curved arrows used to point at details, highlight an element, or create a sense of "look here." Used sparingly — one per post maximum.

Brand Rules

Do's & Don'ts

These rules protect the brand from inconsistency, dilution, and off-brand output. Anyone producing content for or about Respawn should follow these.

Logo & Identity
Do
  • Use the official logo files — white version and black version only
  • Place the logo watermark in the corner of every piece of content we produce
  • Maintain minimum clear space around the logo at all times
  • Scale the logo proportionally — always uniform scaling
Don't
  • Don't use the logo in teal, any gradient, or any color other than black or white
  • Don't add effects — shadows, glows, outlines — to the logo
  • Don't use the wordmark without the rectangular border frame
  • Don't place the logo on low-contrast or visually cluttered backgrounds
Color & Visual
Do
  • Lead with black or white as the dominant background color
  • Use teal for one accent element per design — not more
  • Switch between dark and light mode to keep the feed dynamic
  • Keep photography B&W for editorial, tribute, and portrait posts
Don't
  • Don't use multiple accent colors — teal is the only accent
  • Don't use gradients in Respawn's own brand posts
  • Don't make the background any color other than black, white, or off-white
  • Don't use low-contrast color combinations that reduce legibility
Typography
Do
  • Use the condensed heavy font for all post headlines — big and bold
  • Set headlines in lowercase for a modern, approachable feel
  • Keep leading tight on display text (0.9–1.05 line height)
  • Use the teal label style for category tags and eyebrow text
Don't
  • Don't use casual script or display fonts outside of client work
  • Don't use more than 2 font families in a single post
  • Don't set body text in the condensed display font — it's for headlines only
  • Don't use text effects — 3D, shadows, outlines — on post headlines
Voice & Communication
Do
  • Write like a human who knows their stuff — confident, natural, direct
  • Use Hinglish where it feels natural — don't force English formality
  • Keep captions punchy — one hook, one idea, one CTA maximum
  • Reference culture, memes, and current moments when they're genuinely relevant
Don't
  • Don't write in corporate or formal language — ever
  • Don't use forced meme references that feel out of place
  • Don't pad captions with filler words or unnecessary hashtag spam
  • Don't make promises or claims we can't back up — credibility over hype
